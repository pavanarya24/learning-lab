VENDOR CLAIM · UNTESTED BY US · FAILS TO GENERALIZE

Buy SPY at the close when its 2-day RSI falls below 10 above the 200-day average; sell at the first close above the 5-day average. No stop, no target, median hold 3 days. CRT — Candle Range Theory: Web Research

UNTESTED HYPOTHESIS · NOT A TESTED EDGE

Every candle is a range — its high and low are liquidity pools. Sweep one extreme, close back inside, drop to 15m for a structure shift, enter at OTE (61.8–78.6%), a Breaker or an IFVG, target the opposite level. Web research across 8 published backtests, an automation survey and an origin trace finds the model coherent but unproven: the best-known test wins 62.7% on EUR/USD and 60.1% on gold yet loses −0.05R and −0.10R per trade — random noise wins 62–65% with the same slightly negative expectancy. No published backtest tests the fixed-2R target + 1R partial version built here. Verdict: hypothesis, not an edge — pre-register and backtest before any paper trading.

Use — read the full report before touching the indicator: origin, the 9-step published rules, all 8 backtests, automation options, and a 15-row check of this build against the published rules.

43 · Batch 10 · arXiv:2610.11691

Diffusive Market Impact: A Consistent Microfoundation

IMPACT DECLINE 50–60% · IMPACT RETURNS WHITE

Julius F. Bonart, submitted 8 Oct 2026 (v2 11 Oct). Structural price diffusivity explains the square-root law and its crossover to a linear regime for low trading rates. Microfoundation: a trader plus many arbitrageurs progressively eliminating predictable returns; the impact law is j = U·w, where w is the innovation in the trading schedule and U is causal all-pass — impact returns are white, even though no individual arbitrageur can reconstruct the trading strategy. Under a biexponential trade-flow filter, impact decline is confined to 50%–60%, lower than some empirical estimates. In real markets the impact phase is probably distorted in its long tails, allowing a full relaxation of the impact propagator — weak long-term mean-reversion of the impact state.

Use — execution sizing: if about half the impact decays, do not size as if the full print cost persists; and the white-impact result is why ARE execution costs must be causal, not price-on-flow (see paper 32 — naive bias up to 68%).

42 · Batch 10 · arXiv:2610.05740

Latent Continuum of Regimes in Limit Order Book Dynamics

CONTINUUM, NOT REGIMES · R²_OOS 0.7483

Anjali Thawait, submitted 5 Oct 2026 (v2 6 Oct, 45 pp). Tests whether LOB dynamics have distinct regimes or apparent regimes are discretisations of a continuum: deep LOB data for EURO STOXX 50 index futures, 987 clean trading days 2022–2025, 30 / 45 / 60-second windows. Market states as covariance matrices under Log-Euclidean and affine-invariant geometry; the state cloud has effective dimension ~1.4. A 17-method zoo consistently favours a continuum over a discrete regime structure. One dominant latent coordinate captures 83.66%–85.27% of variation, tracks VSTOXX without regime labels, and stays stable across years. A divisive hierarchy (macro → micro) is evaluated walk-forward: out-of-sample performance is strongest at the macro tier while finer tiers fail to generalise — discretising continuous fine-scale information causes the loss. Forecasting peaks near ten minutes, max pooled R²_OOS 0.7483.

Use — regime work: stop forcing discrete bull / bear / chop labels on the ORB; track one continuous stress coordinate instead. Caveat: EURO STOXX 50 futures, not NQ / ES — validate the coordinate on US index futures before trusting it.

arxiv.org ↗

39 · Batch 9 · arXiv:2611.08528

Price-Volume Correlations Are Always Zero

ρ = 0 BY DEFINITION · CORRELATE SQUARES

Victor Olkhov, submitted 4 Nov 2026 (12 pp, cross-list econ.EM). The usual price–volume correlation, computed from the values, volumes and prices of consecutive trades in an averaging interval, always gives zero by definition — because VWAP depends on mean values and volumes, not on the price time series alone. The standard calculation misleads researchers. The fix the paper derives: to measure statistical dependence between price and volume series, correlate prices with squares of volumes, or squares of prices with squares of volumes. Expressions for the corrected correlations are derived in the paper. No trading rule is claimed — hypothesis only.

Use — a don't-fool-yourself paper for tape reading: any volume/price correlation stat in his own research (VWAP, order-flow entropy, tape filters) must use the corrected form, or it is vacuous.

arxiv.org ↗

38 · Batch 9 · arXiv:2611.08701

Does a Trading Policy Need to Know What It Trades? Microstructure Invariance and Zero-Shot Transfer of Execution Policies

WITHIN 0.015 SPREADS/SHARE · ZERO-SHOT

Hieu Tran & Hong Yu, submitted 5 Nov 2026 (33 pp, 10 figs, cross-list cs.LG). Execution algorithms are normally calibrated asset by asset — queues, trade rates and spreads differ by orders of magnitude. Microstructure invariance says: measured in each asset's own units of time, size and price, markets look alike. Test: exact order-by-order replay of public Nasdaq data. Policies trained once, on 954 stocks in 2019, to choose wait-in-queue vs cross-the-spread, evaluated unchanged on 1,964 unseen stocks in 2020–21, 2,629 stocks in 2025–26, crypto pairs and a second exchange. A policy that sees a stock only through its recent trade rate, trade size, spread and tick costs within 0.015 spreads/share (a fifth of what learning saves) of a policy trained with that stock's own history — the stock's own history does not help it.

Authors' own caveats — invariant units beat raw units, z-scores and asset descriptors by under one basis point in mean cost before fees — most of the advantage is information + a common cost scale a learner can also take as plain inputs; none of the representations tested extrapolates to unseen spread regimes; a near-zero transfer gap to a pooled comparator is weak evidence a representation is right. Use — execution research is his job-hunt target: if invariance holds, one execution policy transfers across his whole US-equities universe instead of per-ticker calibration — and the caveat is exactly his ARE lesson: test on unseen spread regimes, not just unseen tickers.

35 · Batch 7 · MDPI 19(11) 904

Investing in AI Stocks: Machine-Learning Forecasts, Portfolio Strategies, and Stress Tests

SWITCHING SHARPE 5.65 · EQW 0.02

Perry Sadorsky, Schulich School of Business, York University, J. Risk Financial Manag. 2026, 19(11), 904 — published 9 Nov 2026. Daily data Nov 2019–Apr 2026 on four AI sub-sector ETFs: BUG (cybersecurity), CLOU (cloud), BLOK (blockchain), BOTZ (robotics / AI). Tree-based ensembles (bagging best) forecast price direction 1–20 days ahead from technical indicators + business-cycle variables; accuracy 60–74% at medium horizons. Switching portfolio: hold each ETF only when its 5-day bagging forecast is positive; equal-weighted among held ETFs; weekly rebalancing; rolling 80% estimation window re-estimated each horizon; 100 bps trading costs. Test period Mar 2025–Apr 2026 (~14 months, single window): 5-day switching +87.9% annualised return, Sharpe 5.65, Omega 2.66, max drawdown 3.0%; equal-weight +4.5%, Sharpe 0.02, Omega 1.05, max drawdown 26.2%. 10-day results are qualitatively the same. Switching pulls away after Oct 2025 by avoiding drawdowns. Stress test: a 20% market crash cuts switching ~5% vs EQW ~22%. Top SHAP features: 5-yr breakeven inflation, 200-day MA, on-balance volume, Williams A/D — higher inflation expectations and overbought conditions lower the probability of gains.

Authors' own limitation — "The trading evaluation covers a single out-of-sample window of about fourteen months, and the very high Sharpe ratios and smooth equity curves should be confirmed with longer samples." These are reported results, not verification — a 14-month window only. Do not treat the Sharpe 5.65 as a deployable claim.

34 · Batch 6 · arXiv:2610.16151

Neural Estimation of Irreversibility in Real and Simulated Limit Order Book Paths

INCREMENT 0.018 · SIM 0.0006

Yeo, Cha & Hwang, submitted 12 Oct 2026 (v2 15 Oct). A path indistinguishable from its own reverse carries no arrow of time, so irreversibility is a necessary condition for a directed mechanism to be readable from trajectories alone. From three weeks of Nasdaq market-by-order data, NVDA irreversibility is 0.036 / 0.054 nats per event; the ordering increment is 0.018, positive for all twelve symbols on every held-out daytime date, and a surrogate preserving the one-step transition table recovers 30% less. A queue-reactive simulator matches the ordered rate (0.052 vs 0.054) yet scores 0.0006 vs 0.018 on the increment — it builds asymmetry from block composition, not block order.
