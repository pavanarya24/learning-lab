34 · Batch 6 · arXiv:2610.16151

Neural Estimation of Irreversibility in Real and Simulated Limit Order Book Paths

INCREMENT 0.018 · SIM 0.0006

Yeo, Cha & Hwang, submitted 12 Oct 2026 (v2 15 Oct). A path indistinguishable from its own reverse carries no arrow of time, so irreversibility is a necessary condition for a directed mechanism to be readable from trajectories alone. From three weeks of Nasdaq market-by-order data, NVDA irreversibility is 0.036 / 0.054 nats per event; the ordering increment is 0.018, positive for all twelve symbols on every held-out daytime date, and a surrogate preserving the one-step transition table recovers 30% less. A queue-reactive simulator matches the ordered rate (0.052 vs 0.054) yet scores 0.0006 vs 0.018 on the increment — it builds asymmetry from block composition, not block order. Proposed as a benchmark for order-book simulators; code linked from the abstract page. Directly relevant to the Tape Simulator and to paper 28 (Noble LOB sim).

33 · Batch 6 · arXiv:2611.09286

The End-of-Day Reversal After 2019: A Pre-Registered Test on S&P 500 Stocks Excluding Heavily Traded Names

3.13 BPS → 3.05 BPS · 0 IN 2020–22

Vidovich & Zerjal, submitted 6 Nov 2026. Baltussen, Da and Soebhag (2025) documented an end-of-day reversal through Dec 2019: intraday losers beat winners over the last half hour. Tested on later point-in-time S&P 500 members (excluding ~80 heavily traded stocks), under a private pre-registration. Dollar-volume-weighted reversal: 3.13 bps/day (t = 7.5) in 2016–2019 and 3.05 bps (t = 3.0) in 2023–2026 — but indistinguishable from zero in 2020–2022, 2023 carries much of the later estimate, and from 2020 onward it is 1.69 bps. On the full index it falls from 3.40 to 0.74 bps, concentrated in the very largest stocks. No pre-registered mechanism test passes. Relevance is ARE discipline, not a trade to copy: a published anomaly that survives pre-registration in one window and fades in another. Caveat: no claim net of costs; the authors do not trade it.

32 · Batch 6 · arXiv:2610.12537

Market Impact Estimation via Causal Foundation Models

NAIVE BIAS UP TO 68%

Thumm & Chen, submitted 8 Oct 2026. Market impact is causal — what would the price have been had I not traded? — yet classical models (Almgren–Chriss, propagators) regress price on flow, biased whenever informed flow moves prices through information as well as trading. On a synthetic prior the naive estimator's bias is confirmed empirically at up to 68% overestimation, while their causal network tracks the unbiased oracle at high confounding. Zero-shot, it responds at ground-truth scale to metaorders in an ABIDES book (+0.38 pre-onset SD), replicating on ~2,300 flow bursts in BTC/ETH/SOL perpetuals and 3,800 on Nasdaq equities. A recurrent variant raises correlation with measured metaorder impact from +0.68 to +0.92–0.96. Relevance: ARE execution costs regressed price-on-flow may overstate impact by up to 68% when flow is informed. Caveat: proof-of-concept; the authors' own wording is "consistent with, though not proof of" causal transfer.

arxiv.org ↗

31 · Batch 6 · arXiv:2610.28753

Understanding the Inner Workings of a State-Space Foundation Model for Limit Order Books

OFI R² ≈ 0.59

Mohl et al, submitted 20 Oct 2026; accepted ICAIF '26. An 8.2M-parameter Mamba-3 state-space model trained autoregressively on the tokenized message stream of eight large-cap US equities learns an internal representation of the market. Order-flow imbalance, a central driver of short-horizon price moves, is decodable at R² ≈ 0.59, far above controls and baselines. The representation extends to unseen stocks — general, not ticker-specific — and generalises across traded volume and volatility. It is causally steerable: pushing the state along a feature's axis shifts the model's next-message beliefs as that feature implies, monotonically in dose. Directly fits tape reading: OFI is the signal the order-flow platform already watches, and this gives a benchmark — a tape model that cannot decode OFI well above chance has not learned the book.

30 · Batch 5 · arXiv:2609.11614

Deep Learning of Robust Market Making under Regime-Switching Order Flow

BOCPD GATE RESCUES RLMM

Moret & Lillo, submitted 10 Sep 2026. Classical market-making rules (Avellaneda-Stoikov, GLFT) rest on stationary order flow; under regime-switching flow they give negative P&L. The paper builds a deep reinforcement-learning market maker (Rainbow-style distributional DQN, C51) calibrated and tested in a zero-intelligence limit order book. In the stationary setting it outperforms GLFT, but still suffers large drawdowns from inventory saturation under persistent directional imbalance. Augmenting its state with a Bayesian online change-point filter over the directional flow bias, plus a queue-adjusted quote-exposure imbalance, restores profitability. Relevance is the regime filter, not market-making itself: the same BOCPD-over-flow-bias signal that rescues the market maker is the gate his ORB needs. Caveat: results are in a simulated zero-intelligence book, not live markets — do not present the outperformance as a live result.

29 · Batch 5 · arXiv:2601.23172

A unified theory of order flow, market impact, and volatility

H0 ≈ 3/4

Muhle-Karbe, Ouazzani Chahdi, Rosenbaum & Szymanski, submitted 30 Jan 2026 (v2 2 Feb). A microstructural model distinguishing core orders from reaction flow, both Hawkes processes, with a scaling limit that reconciles persistent signed order flow, rough trading volume and volatility, and power-law market impact. All pinned down by a single statistic H0, the persistence of the core flow: signed flow converges to a fractional process with Hurst H0; volume is rough with Hurst H0 − 1/2; volatility is rough with Hurst 2H0 − 3/2; price impact follows a power law with exponent 2 − 2H0. Signed order-flow data give H0 ≈ 3/4 — consistent with the square-root law of market impact. Theoretical, but it gives one estimable number that ties together flow persistence, impact and volatility — the foundation under his order-flow / tape work.

28 · Batch 5 · arXiv:2603.24137

Bridging the Reality Gap in Limit Order Book Simulation

REALISTIC LOB SIM

Noble, Rosenbaum & Souilmi, submitted 25 Mar 2026. A practical, interactive limit-order-book simulator for large-tick assets, designed to produce realistic execution, costs and P&L. The book state is projected onto spread + volume imbalance; event timing reproduces the fine-scale structure of real markets, with a mode at exchange round-trip latency (latency races). A feedback mechanism accumulates signed trade flow through a power-law decay kernel, reproducing concave market impact during execution and partial post-trade reversion. Directly relevant to the Tape Simulator and to ARE backtest realism: a simulator that ignores impact decay and latency races flatters a strategy. Caveat: "several stocks" — no tickers, sample sizes or P&L numbers in the abstract; check the PDF before quoting any.

26 · Batch 4 · arXiv:2611.05218

Beyond Anonymous Order Flow: Wallet Identity as a Feature for Short-Horizon Return Prediction

SPEARMAN ρ = 0.52 · 3.11 BPS

Ding & Zhai, submitted 3 Nov 2026. Reconstructs full-depth books from Hyperliquid's Level-4 record, which exposes persistent pseudonymous wallet addresses, and tests wallet identity as a feature for short-horizon return prediction. The 1–27 Jul 2026 record covers 17.1 billion messages, 14.3 million aggressive orders by 147,113 wallets, and $84.3 billion in taker notional across ten markets. Notional-weighted ten-second markout rankings for wallets persist across adjacent ten-day windows (Spearman ρ = 0.52); covariate-adjusted validation markouts reach 3.11 basis points in the top ventile. Eleven features describing previously scored top-decile wallets lift one-second out-of-sample R² from 10.88% — the abstract cuts off the second figure, so check the PDF before quoting it. Relevance here is tape-reading, not a signal to copy: the portable lesson is persistent-aggressor scoring — rank who is hitting the tape and whether the same aggressors keep winning. Caveat: crypto perpetuals only, on one venue; US equities have no wallet identity, so the feature itself does not port — only the persistence test does.

25 · Batch 4 · arXiv:2611.06607

Hard to Fool: A Bounded LLM Research Loop for Quantitative Finance, and What Two Dozen Campaigns Found

SHARPE ~3 → 0.26 ON HOLDOUT

Zhang, submitted 4 Nov 2026. An open-source agentic research loop built on the opposite premise to most LLM quant loops: the model proposes, and only trusted code decides — proposals are strict declarative JSON over a fixed vocabulary, so there is no model-written code to sandbox. Evaluation runs on immutable chronological stores split into discovery, validation and a final holdout that can be opened exactly once, after a durable receipt is written. Acceptance is a conjunction of absolute gates — positive edge and net P&L after costs, a positive weekly-cluster bootstrap lower bound, survival at elevated costs, per-year stability and sample floors — never a single score. Every campaign run to date is reported, across 0DTE options, overnight SPX verticals, daily ETF rules, technical-analysis premises, Kalshi crypto binaries, earnings options, off-exchange volume and a pre-registered alternative-data campaign; most hypotheses fail, and the failures are reported. The clearest proof the loop works is a failure: an LLM survivor that passed discovery and validation with Sharpe ratios near 3 fell to 0.26 on its single holdout use. Directly validates ARE's design (pre-registered hypotheses, walk-forward + Monte Carlo, worst fold kept) — and sharpens it: ARE's holdout should be opened exactly once, and acceptance should be a conjunction of gates, not one score. Caveat: the abstract says the loop is open-source but gives no repository URL on the abstract page — no repo is linked until one is verified from the paper itself.
